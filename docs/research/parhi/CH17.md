## 1. Concepts, in our words

### 17.1 Why power, and where it goes (pp. 645–648)

- Power became a design metric next to area and time because of portable devices, packaging and cooling cost, and
  reliability (the book quotes failure rates doubling for every 10 °C rise, p. 646).
- CMOS power has standby, leakage, short-circuit and capacitive parts; the capacitive term `α·C_L·V_dd²·f_clk` dominates in
  well-designed circuits (eq. 17.1). α is the switching activity (0→1 transitions per cycle); α·f is the transition density.
  Short-circuit power stays below about 15 % of dynamic power when edges are balanced (p. 648).

### 17.2–17.3 Probabilities and scaling (pp. 648–652)

- Signal, transition and conditional probabilities describe switching; dividing a cycle into time slots of one gate delay
  lets glitches be counted. Scaling devices without scaling V_dd raises power density sharply; lowering V_dd raises delay
  (`∝ V_dd/(V_dd − V_t)²`), and lowering V_t raises leakage tenfold per 80–100 mV, which limits V_t to about 0.3 V (p. 651).

### 17.4 Switching activity and capacitance (pp. 652–661)

- Activity depends on the logic function and on spatial and temporal correlation of the inputs: a 2-input OR gate has
  activity 3/16 with independent inputs, but 1/4, 2/9 or 1/8 under different correlations (p. 653). Glitches add spurious
  transitions; in data-path circuits such as multipliers, glitch power can reach about 40 % (p. 654). In the book's examples,
  activity with glitching is about twice the zero-delay estimate. Dynamic logic switches more but glitches less.
- Capacitance falls with smaller area, shorter wires and fewer devices: resource sharing, common subfunction extraction,
  register sharing (p. 661).

### 17.5 Reduction techniques (pp. 662–671)

- **Path balancing** removes glitches by making paths that meet at a gate arrive together. **Gate and transistor sizing** and
  **pin reordering** trade delay for switched capacitance. **Retiming for low power** places registers so that glitchy nets do
  not drive large capacitances, since register outputs switch less than their inputs (p. 664).
- **Voltage scaling and multiple supplies**: slow blocks off the critical path can run at lower V_dd (example: three supplies
  give about 18 % less power at the same latency, p. 667); **dual/multi-V_t** (MTCMOS, VTCMOS, EVTCMOS) cut standby leakage.
- **Clocking**: the clock is the one signal that always switches; derived clocks that are slowed or stopped for idle blocks
  (clock gating, activity-driven clock trees) and dual-edge flip-flops save power (pp. 668–670). Asynchronous design is the
  limit case of clock gating.
- **Circuit styles**: pass-transistor families (CPL, SRPL, DCVS) need fewer transistors than static CMOS for the same function.

### 17.6 Power estimation (pp. 671–688)

- Simulation-based (circuit, switch or gate level; Monte Carlo with a sample-size formula, eq. 17.49) versus non-simulative
  (library models per functional unit, word-level probability densities, entropy bounds, signal probabilities with BDDs,
  transition density by Boolean differences). The book's own hierarchical tool (HEAT) models sub-circuits as state-transition
  diagrams, solves their Markov chains for edge activities, and weights SPICE-characterised edge energies.

### 17.7–17.8 Conclusions and problems (pp. 688–694)

- At algorithm level, approximate processing and dynamic algorithm changes (shorter filters, shorter words when the
  signal-to-noise ratio allows) also save power.

## 3. What this means for SENTINEL

1. **Only α, C and f are available.** SENTINEL may not change any voltage (`CLAUDE.md` section 8), and VCCINT comes from the
   board. Overclocking raises f and therefore dynamic power roughly in proportion; the governor and the XADC temperature
   reading are the guard (`CH03.md` item 4, `CH16.md` item 6). The official reports include Vivado's power estimate
   (`parse_power` in `fpga/run_official.py`), which is a static estimate, not a measurement.
2. **Skipping zero activations is compatible with the checksums** [INFERENCE, needs a [SIM] measurement]. Because one
   activation is broadcast to all lanes (`docs/SPEC.md` 5.2), a zero activation makes every data-lane product *and* every
   checksum-lane product `wc_c · a` zero in the same cycle. Skipping that cycle leaves every data sum and every checksum sum
   unchanged, so detection, localization and correction work as before, and the results stay bit-exact. After ReLU and with
   zero padding, many activations are zero, so this would cut cycles (speed) and switching (power). The share of zero
   activations per layer for the deployed model is [UNKNOWN] here and can be measured with the golden model on the test
   images before anything is proposed. Design points to settle: the "first operand of a unit" flag must move to the first
   issued operand, a unit whose operands are all zero must still produce zero, and the cycle model and `CYCLES_TOTAL`
   checks would change (ADR).
3. **Glitch power is another argument for DSP lanes** [INFERENCE]. Multipliers built from LUTs glitch (p. 654); a DSP slice
   with its internal pipeline register stops glitches at the slice. If power matters, this adds to the timing reason in
   `CH13.md` item 2.
4. **Clock enables for idle lanes** [INFERENCE]. Null lanes (`CH06.md` item 2) and idle phases between layers could hold
   their registers with clock enables. Vivado has a power-optimisation step that adds such enables automatically; whether
   it helps this design is [UNKNOWN] until a run compares the power reports. The contest rules do not list power as a
   criterion (`docs/official/README.md`), so this is low priority.
5. **Approximate processing is ruled out** for the same reason as relaxed look-ahead (`CH10.md` item 3): it changes results.

Candidates noted for the development list: zero-activation skipping (Should, after a [SIM] count of zero activations; needs
an ADR); DSP lanes (already listed); power-optimisation run (Could).
