## 1. Concepts, in our words

### 14.1–14.2 Redundant number systems (pp. 529–531)

- A radix-r signed-digit system with more than r digit values (e.g. {−1, 0, 1} for radix 2) gives several representations
  per number. Its key property is **carry-free addition**: a carry moves at most two or three positions, so addition time
  does not depend on the word length. This makes most-significant-digit-first ("on-line") arithmetic possible, which two's
  complement cannot do; it suits division, square root and low-latency recursive filters (p. 529).
- **Carry-save** is also a redundant form (digit values 0, 1, 2), and most redundant designs carry over to it (p. 530).
- Redundancy factor ρ = α/(r − 1): minimally redundant (α = r/2), maximally redundant (α = r − 1), over-redundant (α > r − 1)
  (Table 14.1).

### 14.3–14.4 Carry-free adders (pp. 531–540)

- A radix-2 signed digit is coded as two bits, x = x⁺ − x⁻. **Hybrid** addition (redundant + ordinary binary) is done in
  two steps: each position forms a transfer digit and an interim sum independently, then each sum digit combines the
  interim sum with the transfer from the right; one "plus-plus-minus" (PPM) full-adder cell per digit. Hybrid subtraction
  uses "minus-minus-plus" (MMP) cells; a full signed-digit addition is a hybrid addition followed by a hybrid subtraction,
  at twice the hardware of the hybrid adder/subtractor. Digit-serial versions come from folding (chapter 6): the
  lsd-first adder has zero latency, the msd-first adder one or two clocks.
- Radix-4: a maximally redundant hybrid adder is the same structure as a radix-2 one of twice the length; a minimally
  redundant one needs a local carry inside each cell.

### 14.5 On-line multipliers (pp. 540–545)

- Bit-serial msd-first multipliers of a redundant and a two's complement operand, obtained by systolic mapping of two
  multiplication dependence graphs. The better one has a critical path of one adder and a fixed latency of 3 clocks
  independent of the word length, with 2 registers per stage (bit-level pipelined two's complement bit-serial multipliers
  need at least 3) (pp. 544–545).

### 14.6–14.7 Format conversion (pp. 545–551)

- Ordinary to redundant: trivial (radix 2) or nearly so (radix 4). Redundant to ordinary needs a carry propagation, because
  the lowest digit can change every higher digit (example on p. 547). Lsd-first: a ripple of MMP cells, like a binary
  adder. Msd-first: keep two candidate results (for carry-in 0 and −1) and pick one at each step (COPY and APPEND cells).

### 14.8–14.9 Conclusions and problems (pp. 551–557)

- Fast converters can borrow carry-select or tree carry schemes (appendix E). Problems: carry-save hybrid adders, radix-4
  adders, converters, sign detection, msd-first binary addition through a redundant-to-binary converter.

## 3. What this means for SENTINEL

1. **On an FPGA, two-operand adds are already fast** [INFERENCE]. The Spartan-7 fabric has dedicated carry chains (the
   CARRY4 cells counted in every timing path of `results/vivado/official/impl_timing_summary.rpt`). A 32-bit binary add
   uses eight of them in a row, and the accumulator path is not critical (`CH02.md`). Redundant two-operand adders built
   from LUTs give up the carry chain and are usually not faster at these widths. So redundant arithmetic is not proposed for
   the lane accumulators.
2. **Carry-save accumulators, as a fallback only** [INFERENCE]. If the accumulator loop ever limits the clock, keeping
   `acc` as a sum vector and a carry vector makes each step one 3:2 compression (one LUT level) with no carry propagation;
   one final carry-propagate add per unit (every T ≥ 27 cycles) turns it back into binary and can be pipelined. Exact
   integer arithmetic, so bit-exact. Cost: about twice the accumulator flip-flops and one compressor per bit per lane. It
   joins the two-accumulator and look-ahead fallbacks of `CH02.md`, `CH04.md` and `CH10.md`; none is needed now.
3. **Where carry-save does help: multi-operand sums.** The C3 checksum-weight sum of 14 shifted terms (`CH13.md` item 1) is
   a multi-operand addition; a carry-save (Wallace-type) tree with one final carry-propagate add, or a tree of three-input
   adders, is the standard way to cut its depth. This is the same candidate as in `CH13.md`, with this chapter as the
   reason carry-save fits there and not in the accumulators.
4. **A remark for the research story, not a claim** [INFERENCE]. Under overclocking, the longest carry paths are the first
   that static timing says will run out of time. Moving long carry chains out of loops (carry-save inside, one merge adder
   at the end that could run with margin) would change where timing errors can appear. Whether that matters on the board is
   [UNKNOWN] and would need `[HW]` measurements (E-series experiments), so it stays an idea.

Candidates noted for the development list: carry-save tree for the C3 sum (same as `CH13.md`); carry-save accumulators
(Could, fallback only).
