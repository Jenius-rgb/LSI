## 1. Concepts, in our words

### 5.1 What unfolding is (pp. 119–121)

- Unfolding by a factor J rewrites a program so that one new iteration does J consecutive old iterations
  (loop unrolling in compilers). Example: `y(n) = a·y(n−9) + x(n)` becomes a pair of equations for y(2k) and
  y(2k+1) (eq. 5.2). In the unfolded graph each delay is J-slow: it holds a value for J old iterations.
- Uses: expose hidden concurrency so a schedule can reach a shorter iteration period; build word-parallel
  architectures from word-serial ones; build bit-parallel and digit-serial architectures from bit-serial ones.

### 5.2 The algorithm (pp. 121–124)

- For every node U draw J copies U_0 … U_{J−1}. For every edge U → V with w delays draw the J edges
  `U_i → V_{(i+w) % J}` with `⌊(i+w)/J⌋` delays, i = 0 … J−1 (p. 121). Example: an edge with 9 delays,
  J = 2, becomes edges with 4 and 5 delays.
- An edge with w < J delays becomes J − w edges without delay and w edges with one delay. The construction
  keeps every precedence constraint of J iterations (proof pp. 123–124).

### 5.3 Properties (pp. 124–126)

- The total number of delays stays the same: `Σ_i ⌊(w+i)/J⌋ = w` (eq. 5.3).
- A loop with w_l delays becomes `gcd(w_l, J)` loops, each with `w_l / gcd` delays and `J / gcd` copies of
  every node of the loop (property 5.3.2).
- The iteration bound of the unfolded graph is J·T∞ (property 5.3.3): the same bound per original sample.

### 5.4 Critical path, unfolding and retiming (pp. 127–128)

- A path with J or more delays cannot become critical after J-unfolding (corollary 5.4.1). To make the
  J-unfolded graph meet a clock period c, retime the original graph with the constraint
  `r(U) − r(V) ≤ W(U,V) − J` whenever `D(U,V) > c`, plus the usual feasibility constraint.
- Retiming before unfolding can reach any clock period that retiming after unfolding can (lemma 5.4.1).

### 5.5 Applications (pp. 128–140)

- **Reaching the iteration bound** (pp. 128–131). Two cases need unfolding: a node longer than T∞ (use
  `⌈t_U / T∞⌉`-unfolding; Fig. 5.6, T∞ = 3 with 4-unit nodes reaches 3 per sample after 2-unfolding), and a
  non-integer bound `t_l / w_l` (use w_l-unfolding; Fig. 5.7, T∞ = 4/3 after 3-unfolding). A graph with one
  delay in every loop (perfect-rate) can always be scheduled at its bound; unfolding by the lcm of the loop
  delay counts always gives a perfect-rate graph, but it is an upper bound, not the minimum.
- Schedules for programmable DSPs: non-overlapped schedules have the period of the critical path of the
  acyclic precedence graph; overlapped ones can be shorter but are harder to build (Figs. 5.8–5.9: bound 3.5,
  non-overlapped period 6, 2-unfolded overlapped period 7 = 3.5 per iteration).
- **Word-level parallel processing** (pp. 131–133): J-unfolding a word-serial architecture gives a
  J-parallel one that takes J words per clock (Fig. 5.10, a 3-parallel FIR filter); the block filters of
  chapter 3 are a special case.
- **Bit-level** (pp. 134–140): bit-serial (one bit per clock, a word in W clocks), bit-parallel (a word per
  clock) and digit-serial (N bits per clock, W/N clocks per word). Bit-serial circuits contain switches
  (multiplexers) that act at given bit positions. To unfold a switch acting at `W·l + u` with `W = W'·J`:
  write `W·l + u = J(W'·l + ⌊u/J⌋) + (u % J)` and draw the edge `U_{u%J} → V_{u%J}` switched at
  `W'·l + ⌊u/J⌋` (p. 135). A bit-serial adder unfolded by 4 becomes a ripple-carry adder, by 2 a digit-serial
  adder. If J does not divide W, expand the switch times to period lcm(W, J); a 3-bit-digit adder for 4-bit
  words then handles 3 words every 4 clocks at the price of more control. With switches, the delay count is
  not preserved.

### 5.6–5.7 Conclusions and problems (pp. 140–148)

- Unfolding reveals concurrency, gives word-parallel designs for speed or power, and cuts cycles per sample on
  programmable DSPs. Problems include unrolling an FIR filter on a 3-MAC processor with one data bus
  (problem 17), minimum unfolding factors, and digit-serial adders.

## 3. What this means for SENTINEL

1. **Unfolding the MAC loop = more operands per lane per cycle** [INFERENCE]. Today a lane takes one
   (activation, weight) pair per cycle and needs T = Cin·9 cycles per conv unit (`docs/SPEC.md` 5.2). Unfolding
   the accumulation by J = 2 gives each lane two products per cycle summed into one accumulator, so T halves.
   Property 5.3.3 says the loop bound per product does not improve (the adder chain in the loop doubles), so
   the gain comes from the second multiplier, not from the loop. Costs: J multipliers per lane (the 77 unused
   DSP slices could carry them, `CH03.md`), and J times the operand bandwidth (J activations and J weight
   words per cycle from block RAM). Bit-exact: integer addition is associative. Checksums: C1, C2, C3 are
   linear in the products, so a checksum lane that also takes J products per cycle keeps the relation per unit.
2. **Unfolding versus more lanes** [INFERENCE]. Both double the MACs. More lanes (larger K) keep one
   activation per cycle and widen only the weight path; unfolding widens the activation path too, and its
   gain depends on Cin being a multiple of J (else a zero-padded tail, as lanes already do for Cout not a
   multiple of K, SPEC 5.1). Larger K looks simpler for SENTINEL; both need an implementation run.
3. **Bit-serial or digit-serial lanes: not a fit now** [INFERENCE]. They trade lanes for cycles and suit
   variable precision. SENTINEL is fixed INT8 with bit-exact vectors and a January freeze; rebuilding the
   lanes as digit-serial units would touch every bench. Recorded only for completeness.
4. **No node longer than the loop bound sits inside a loop.** The long node (the LUT multiplier) is
   feed-forward (`CH02.md`), so section 5.5.1's reason for unfolding does not arise; pipelining
   (`CH03.md`) is the right tool for it.

Candidates noted for the development list: two products per lane per cycle (Could, weighed against a larger
K); digit-serial lanes (not proposed).
