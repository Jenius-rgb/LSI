## SENTINEL's answer to the reasons of Marty et al.

The report must answer all three reasons with data, not with claims:

1. *Two or more errors frequently occur.* Marty et al. examine one checksum per tile (hundreds to thousands of outputs). SENTINEL examines per unit, namely one output pixel for K channels at once, so the probability of two errors in a single check is much smaller at the same frequency [INFERENCE]. Two errors in one unit are still recognized by C3 and fall back to recalculation, not miscorrected. How often two lanes fail simultaneously is hypothesis H1 and measured in E1; there is no [HW] data yet.
2. *Recalculation is not expensive.* True for low error rates (their formula T = N/O + S·N·E). In-place correction provides benefits in the intermediate error rate region, where recalculation begins to consume the gains. E4 compares mode 1 (detect-then-recalculate, like Marty et al.) and mode 2 (correction) on the same bitstream and board. If correction does not win, that is reported.
3. *Recovery logic is expensive.* SENTINEL's checker and corrector costs are measured, not estimated: official synthesis report [VIVADO-STA] and variant without C2 for area comparison (SPEC §8).

Their finding that deserves attention: observed errors are mostly in the LSB, contrary to the common assumption that upper bits fail first (Chamberlin et al. 2024). SENTINEL's localization works for any bit pattern, but the E1 atlas must record bit positions so they can be compared.

## Claims that may be written (if data is present)

- Timing error atlas for a real CNN on small-class FPGA (Spartan-7), per frequency, layer, lane, and bit.
- Zero-stall single-lane error correction, compared directly against detect-then-recalculate a la Marty et al. on the same design and board.
- Per-layer clock selection on a single engine with a governor entirely inside the FPGA, without CPU. Marty et al. control frequency from software per tile, and frequency per layer is only mentioned by them for streaming accelerators with separate clock managers per layer.

## Claims that must not be written

- "First" or "novel" for ABFT, dual checksums, or CNN overclocking. All of them already exist.
- Anything about timing error behavior derived solely from simulation or synthetic models.
